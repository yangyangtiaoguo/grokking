## Gap Summary

The paper's defensible core is **C1 (the phase diagram itself) and C2 (the low-dose rescue with mechanism-isolating controls)** — both have substantial, carefully-controlled evidence and answer a real, previously-unaddressed question (Zhao's paper showed a coarse two-point version of the stratification effect; this paper provides the continuous axis, the boundary structure, and the dose-response characterization Zhao's paper does not attempt).

**C3, C4, and C6 are genuinely partial contributions, not clean wins**, and the paper should not pretend otherwise:
- C3 survived an internal rejection and reached "partial" only after substantial additional seeds — it should ride on C1's structure as supporting evidence, not stand alone.
- C4 explicitly failed to uniquely resolve a governing law (two candidates remain indistinguishable out-of-sample) — its contribution is methodological (a working example of pre-registered held-out testing for boundary shape) more than substantive.
- C6 is split: the modular-addition result is solid; the Dyck-1 result is an honest partial disconfirmation of full task-generality that the paper must report as such, not soften into ambiguity.

**C5 is a solid but narrow methodological contribution** — good practice demonstrated (pre-specified predictions with provenance), not a major scientific finding on its own.

**Overinflation risk assessment**: the single greatest risk to this paper's credibility under review is drifting any of C1, C3, or C6 back toward the original (pre-audit) framing — "phase transition" language for C1, "proven divergence/critical slowing down" for C3, or "task-general/universal mechanism" for C6. All three were explicitly narrowed by two independent rounds of audit specifically because the original framing outran the evidence. The paper's actual strength is that it is honest about exactly how far the evidence goes — this should be treated as a selling point in Discussion ("we show precisely where a plausible-sounding generalization holds and where it breaks down"), not hedged apologetically.
